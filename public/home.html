The project

Mapping the worlds of the undead

From the strigoi of Romanian fiction to the vampires and revenants of the Gothic and pulp traditions, the undead are among literature's most persistent figures. Undead Networks studies them with the tools of the digital humanities: each work becomes structured data that we read at a distance, alongside close reading of the text itself.

The aim is not to replace reading with numbers, but to raise new questions: where the undead stand in their worlds, who mediates between the living and the dead, and how Romanian and international traditions imagine the revenant differently.

01

Character networks

Weighted graphs of dialogue and co-presence, with centrality measures, communities and gender.

02

Vocabulary profiles

Lexical diversity, part-of-speech patterns and the words each character keeps for particular partners.

03

Narrative events

Acts extracted from the narration, such as predation, with agent, patient and sense (literal or figurative).

04

Translation comparison

Aligning two translations of the same work, dialogue by dialogue, and comparing their tone.

How it works

Machines count, researchers decide

Computation handles the large, repetitive work. People make the interpretive calls a machine cannot make reliably.

Full methodology →

  1. Extract

    Every line of direct speech, and every candidate act in the narration, is pulled from the digitised text.

    Automatic

  2. Validate & annotate

    Researchers check each line and record speaker, addressee and silent witnesses; each candidate event gets its agent, patient and sense.

    Researcher

  3. Model

    The annotations become networks, centralities, communities and vocabulary profiles, with every correction logged.

    Automatic

  4. Read back

    The interactive views send readers back to specific passages, to test what the graph suggests against the text.

    Researcher
